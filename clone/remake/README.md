# clone meets clone by Acme and Fudge (Takeover '98) — high resolution remake

A C++/OpenGL re-implementation of *clone meets clone*, the MS-DOS 64k intro by Acme and Fudge (design Aap and Echo,
effects Echo, music Simon, graphics Aap; texture mapper and grid filler Craft, radial blur originally Dune) that won
the PC 64k intro competition at Takeover '98. It runs on Linux and Windows at any resolution instead of 320×200
(the original already draws in true colour), with the original's motion, timing and music, and with high-resolution
redraws of Aap's pictures (or the original pixels, `--original-art`).

The remake reads everything from the original `CLONE.EXE` (63,994 bytes), in the original release in [`clone/original/`](../original/).

| what | where in the exe | how it is used |
|------|------------------|----------------|
| the program image | a "VE" container behind PMODE/W and a small LE loader ("veloader"): LZ77 with canonical Huffman codes, a fixup list | unpacked at start-up by `src/unpack.cpp` (as the veloader does it, 1 ms) and checked against the known release by hash |
| the music | an MXM module in the image, completed by 20 samples the intro synthesises at start-up | built by `src/modsynth.cpp`, played by a port of its MXMPlay build (`src/mxmplay.cpp`) through a model of the GUS GF1 (`src/gf1.cpp`) |
| the running order | the sync table 0x420499 (98 entries: music positions → events) | the part events and the events inside the parts as music times (`src/timeline.cpp`) |
| the pictures | four captions, four glyphs, the title and the credits: 8-bit pixels with three grey palettes | the redraws in `art/`, or the original pixels (`src/pictures.cpp`) |
| the textures | generated by the intro from formulas (three maps, two colour tables) | evaluated exactly at high resolution from the constants in the image (`src/clone/textures.cpp`) |
| the rest | tables at image addresses: the tile colours, the random seed, the octahedron of the geosphere, the cube tables, the parts' constants | read by address where the parts need them |

All addresses are linear addresses of the program relocated for 0x400000; the notes in [`../re/`](../re/) explain
every one of them.

## Running

The program looks for `CLONE.EXE` next to itself, in its parent folders, and in an `original` folder in any of
those, so `clone/original/CLONE.EXE` is found from the build folder; or pass `--exe FILE`. The redraws are found the
same way in an `art` folder (`clone/remake/art`); or pass `--art DIR`.

```
clone-remake [--window] [--size 1280x960] [--start SECONDS] [--ss 2] [--loop] [--info] [--original-art] [--novsync]
```

Keys: `Esc` quit, `Space` pause, `←/→` seek ±5 s, `↓/↑` seek ±30 s, `F`/`F11` toggle fullscreen.
`--ss 2` renders at twice the resolution and downsamples. `--info` shows the time, music position, part and main's
time t70 in the title bar. `--original-art` shows the original pixels of the pictures instead of the redraws.

Offline: `--wav out.wav` writes the soundtrack (48 kHz stereo); `--shots 10,62.5 --shotdir DIR --shotsize WxH`
writes the frames at those music times (seconds) to PNG (the folder is created); `--video out.mp4 --from T0 --to T1
[--fps 60] [--shotsize WxH]` renders a range with sound through `ffmpeg` (the whole intro without `--from` /
`--to`); `--bench T0,T1 --shotsize WxH [--fps F]` reports the GPU time per frame (with `--fps` at the steps of
playback at F frames per second); `--cues` prints the running order with the capture's ticks; `--picshots all
--shotdir DIR` draws the ten pictures (redraw or original pixels) to PNG, `--picshots mapA,mapB,mapC,table1,table2`
the generated textures.

## Building

Requirements: a C++20 compiler, CMake ≥ 3.20 and SDL3. If SDL3 is not installed, CMake downloads and builds it
(3.2.24); `-DCLONE_FETCH_DEPS=ON` always does that. No other libraries are used (`stb_image` and `stb_image_write`
are included in `third_party`).

**Linux (Fedora)**

```
sudo dnf install gcc-c++ cmake ninja-build SDL3-devel
cmake -S . -B build -G Ninja && cmake --build build
./build/clone-remake --window
```

On Fedora Atomic or without root, the same works inside a toolbox.

**Windows from Linux (MinGW cross-compile, Fedora)**

```
sudo dnf install mingw64-gcc-c++ mingw64-SDL3 mingw64-winpthreads-static
mingw64-cmake -S . -B build-win -G Ninja && cmake --build build-win
```

This gives `build-win/clone-remake.exe`; it needs `SDL3.dll` (from the MinGW sysroot). Put `CLONE.EXE` next to them
or in an `original` folder next to them, and the `art` folder next to them. (The program is called `clone-remake`
so it can never overwrite `CLONE.EXE`.)

**Windows (Visual Studio 2022)**: `cmake -S . -B build` then `cmake --build build --config Release` (SDL3 is built
from source). Only the MinGW build has been tried.

`-DCLONE_PARTS="tube;zoom"` builds only some of the parts in `src/scenes` (development); the others show a
placeholder with the part's name and number, the music position, the part's clock, the routine, the capture's tick
and the part's captions.

## How it is put together

- `src/main.cpp` options, playback loop, shots / video / bench; `src/demo.*` the framework (parts, the clocks, the
  sync events, `TickStepper`, render-target pool, placeholders); `src/timeline.cpp` the running order;
  `src/player.*` playback and the clock (the audible position of the soundtrack)
- `src/exe.*`, `src/unpack.cpp` reading and unpacking `CLONE.EXE`; `src/pictures.*` the ten pictures: the redraws in
  `art/` or the original pixels through their grey palettes
- `src/music.*`, `src/modsynth.*`, `src/mxmplay.*`, `src/gf1.*` the soundtrack, rendered at start-up
- `src/clone/` the core library (namespace `cmc`, as "clone" is a POSIX function): `core.h` (the random generator
  0x420491, the log curves and their gains, the tile colours, the overlay fades, palette textures), `textures.h`
  (the five generated textures), `engine.h` (the 3D engine and Craft's texture mapper on the GPU)
- `src/scenes/` one port per part: `swarm` (part 1, both runs), `tiles` (11), `tube` (2), `blob` (8), `ring` (6),
  `cubes` (9), `zoom` (5), `bumps` (7), `dots` (3), `tunnels` (4), `slabs` (10); `grid.h` is Craft's grid filler
  (parts 2, 5, 4), `tiles3d.h` the tile band and the cubes of parts 7..10, `particles.h` and `particles_sim.h` the
  stamps, Dune's radial blur and the particle state of parts 1, 6 and 3, `particle_frames.h` the capture's frame
  times for them
- `art/` the redrawn pictures and how they were made (`art/README.md`); `tools/align_art.py` fits a redraw onto the
  original
- `tools/compare.py` puts remake frames next to the original's capture; `tools/test_music.cpp` checks the soundtrack
  (`cmake --build build --target test_music`); `tools/enginetest/` checks the 3D engine against the draws dumped from
  the emulator; `tools/particlecheck.cpp` drives the original's 8-bit particle pipeline with the remake's particle
  state; `tools/particle_frames.py` makes `particle_frames.h` from the capture
- [`../re/PORT.md`](../re/PORT.md) the conventions every part follows; `../re/` the decompiled notes (pseudo-C with
  every constant), the emulator harness and the scripts that make the reference capture

## Fidelity

Every routine was decompiled, modelled in Python and checked against the original running in an emulator (see
[`../re/README.md`](../re/README.md)); the ports are compared with a capture of the original made in that emulator,
which runs the intro at 150 million instructions per second, a little faster than the 133 MHz Pentium the intro
asks for, in its 32-bit video mode.

- **Timing.** The clock is the music: music time t = xmpGetTimer / 1,193,182 s, 0 at the player's second timer
  interrupt, when row 00:00 starts to sound. The player ticks at 53.99 Hz (135 BPM, speed 3: a row every
  55.57 ms). Main reads the music position (one player tick ahead of the sound) once per frame and runs the sync
  table's events; the remake starts each part at its event's music time and ends the intro at 216.910 s, as the
  capture does. `--cues` prints the running order.
- **Motion.** No part reads the timer. Main computes its time t70 = timer / 17043 after every shown frame (the unit
  is 1/70.0101 s: the authors took the timer chip for 1,193,010 Hz), and every part integrates its variables by
  dt = the change of t70 once per drawn frame. A variable that moves linearly is a closed form of the part's clock,
  which starts where the capture's did, at the timer of the previous part's last frame (0..17 ms before the event);
  the remake evaluates those closed forms at t, so motion is smooth at any refresh rate. Part 1 starts with the
  image's dt of 6.0, and its second run continues the first run's clock.
- **Frame-rate dependent state.** The intro has no retrace wait and draws as fast as the CPU allows, 26 to 725
  frames per second in the capture. What it advances once per drawn frame (the particle parts' spawns, targets,
  trails, part 3's magnify-and-darken) is stepped at the capture's own frame times and blended between them; where
  an event's effect or a lag depends on which frame saw it (part 2's camera, part 5's direction flips, part 9's axis
  switches, part 7's freeze, part 4's pulse and rate switches), the remake uses the capture's frames. Details below
  and under "Known differences".
- **Randomness.** The random generator 0x420491 is replayed from its seed in the image; one sequence runs through
  parts 1, 6, 3 and part 1's second run, so their particles are the capture's.
- **Music.** The module is built from the image as the intro builds it (the 20 synthesised samples bit for bit,
  with the x87's truncating arithmetic), and the player's GUS register writes, the GUS memory after the upload and
  the music position after every timer interrupt match the capture exactly (`tools/test_music.cpp`). The GF1 model
  evaluates the voices at the output rate (48 kHz) instead of the card's 34,300 frames per second with 18 voices,
  with the card's pitch, volume ramps and timing; the player's volume reads are answered from the card model, as
  real hardware would. A 5 Hz high-pass stands for the card's AC-coupled output, and the mix is normalised to a peak
  of −1 dBFS (the player's master volume 0x20 leaves each voice a quarter of full scale).
- **Pictures.** The ten stored pictures are shown as faithful high-resolution redraws by default (see below);
  `--original-art` shows the original pixels as sharp squares with anti-aliased edges. The blend levels of the
  title and credits fades are continuous.
- **Sharper.** Effects are evaluated per output pixel; the generated textures are evaluated from their formulas at
  high resolution and read filtered; 8-bit intensity buffers are float; the log / exp tables, the 64ths of the
  multiply table and the 16 × 16 weight tables are float maths; repeated box blurs are Gaussians of the same
  variance; edges are anti-aliased.
- **Log-encoded colour.** The textures store brightness as 8-bit logarithms (L = 128 + K ln V) and the fillers add a
  shade to L and decode it with an exp table, so the shade multiplies the colour and the clamp at 255 turns bright
  areas white. The remake filters the levels in the log domain as the fillers do, adds the shade and decodes with
  the exponential, keeping the clamp.
- **Grid filler** (parts 2, 5, 4; `src/scenes/grid.h`). Craft's filler evaluates each part's texture coordinates
  and light at 41 × 21 grid points and interpolates them over 8 × 8 blocks; the remake evaluates each part's
  function at every pixel. The grid texture is the original's 256 × 256 texels with the integer cell radius (the
  texel style: square-ish cells with thin dark borders, as the original shows them), filtered in the log domain and
  mipmapped where the original minifies it.
- **3D engine** (parts 7..10; `src/clone/engine.h`). Geometry in float as the original's (it is float already),
  rasterised with 4× MSAA and a depth buffer, perspective-correct per pixel, Craft's bilinear map read in the log
  form with continuous weights, the shade interpolated and decoded with the exponential. The maps are evaluated in
  the smooth style (the continuous cell radius at 4 texels per original texel: round spots). The far cut that makes
  part 8's blob come in face by face is kept.
- **Particles** (parts 1, 6, 3; `src/scenes/particles.h`). The 8-bit buffers are float intensity targets at up to
  1280 × 960, coloured per pixel with the palettes' formula min(255, C v / 92); the 4 × 4 stamps are smooth sprites
  of the same footprint and integral. Dune's radial blur runs in place from the centre outwards, so each pixel adds
  a sample of pixels the pass has already brightened: the remake solves that recursion on the GPU in the original's
  order (below).

## The art switch and the redraws

The remake shows faithful high-resolution redraws of the ten pictures Aap drew for the intro: the four captions
("meet us", "get them clones", "connect the dots", "tunnels **meet** more tunnels"), the four brush-drawn glyphs,
the title "clone **meets** clone" and the credits ".aap .echo .simon". They are AI redraws made with ChatGPT's image
generation from the original pixels and fitted onto them, the words and lines one by one;
[`art/README.md`](art/README.md) says how each was made and how closely it fits. `--original-art` shows the
original pixels instead; a part never knows which it draws.

- The texts are white letters whose grey is their coverage, so over the black boxes they look like the original;
  over part 1's particles the title and the credits have no dark rim, where the original's anti-aliasing pixels are
  opaque grey.
- The glyphs are grey pictures with an alpha channel where the original's index 0 is transparent.
- Everything else on screen is generated (the textures, the tile colours, the particles) and stays computed from the
  original.

## Checks

Numbers from the final check (Linux, i7-13700H, music time in seconds):

- **Against the capture**: contact sheets of the remake (both art modes) next to the capture every 8 s over the
  whole intro (`tools/compare.py`), and of every part boundary (30 ms before and 40 ms after each start, the first
  and last frames). Mean absolute difference to the capture (320×200, 0..255) with the original pixels: 0.3 for the
  title mosaic, 0.7–3.8 for part 1, 1.4–5.2 for the grid parts, 1.8–4.0 for the 3D parts, 2.6–2.9 for part 6, 6–9
  for part 3's cloud (smoother than the original's); with the redraws 0.4–9.5. Every part boundary shows what the
  capture shows (at most 8.4, in moving pictures).
- **Seeking**: 15 times across all parts rendered in ascending and in descending order give identical frames
  (maximum difference 0). 48 frames played at 60 fps in each part and a jump to the last of them give identical
  frames, except in part 1's two runs, where the trails rebuilt after a jump differ by at most 1/255.
- **Judder**: 48 consecutive frames at 60 fps at a moment of every part change smoothly from frame to frame (no
  repeated frames where something moves; the change per frame varies by at most 13 % from one frame to the next, 9 %
  in nine steps out of ten, apart from part 9's axis switch and part 10's light pulse). Part 6's dots blink at the
  capture's 28.9 frames per second, so every second frame at 60 fps also shows the dots that went out and came on (a
  change of 1.1 instead of 0.62); the motion between is even. At 35 s part 11's tiles have finished fading in and
  the picture hardly changes.
- **Speed**: GPU time per frame at 2560×1920 over each whole part at the steps of 60 fps playback (`--bench T0,T1
  --fps 60`), median / 95th percentile in ms. Played through, the whole intro has no frame slower than its first on
  the Iris Xe (30 ms); on zink the first frames of some parts and one frame of part 1 take 10–100 ms. After a jump
  the first frame rebuilds what the part needs: 14–35 ms, 90 ms in part 1, 140 ms in part 3 and 195 ms in part 1's

## Known differences

- **The start-up** (the text-mode title, "samples [....] - uploading" and "textures [....]" while the module and
  the textures are built, 0.65 s in the emulator) is skipped: the remake starts with the music.
- **The 32-bit mode.** The original also runs in a 15-bit mode, converting its 32-bit buffer in software (with
  tearing); the remake follows the 32-bit mode, the one the capture shows. Rows a part does not write show, in the
  32-bit mode, the page drawn four frames earlier, which is black in the capture at every part: they are black.
- **Events.** Part changes and sync events happen at their music time; the original runs them at the first frame
  after it (up to one frame later, 1.4..38 ms in the capture). Where the capture's lag changes the picture, the
  remake keeps it (below). The intro ends at 216.910 s, after the frame that sees the last row (interrupt 11713 as
  in the capture; on a real machine it depends on the frame rate).
- **The title and the credits redrawn** carry their grey as coverage of white: over the particles of part 1 the
  title has no dark rim (the original's anti-aliasing pixels are opaque).
- **Grid parts (11, 2, 5, 4).** Evaluated per pixel instead of on the original's 8 × 8 blocks, the grid texture
  mipmapped where the original shows block noise (part 5's strong zoom, the far ends of the tunnels). The original's
  truncations (texture levels, light, the bilinear sum, the decode: about 1.6 levels of 4.4 %) are replaced by their
  mean, so the colours match on average. The 0.001 guard in the original's cylinder distance makes a bright dot of
  under one original pixel at a tunnel's vanishing point, which the original's grid hardly ever samples; it is left
  out. Part 11's tile colours are not truncated to bytes and their edges are anti-aliased.
- **Frame-rate effects of the grid parts, kept as the capture shows them**: part 2's camera position one frame
  (24.7 ms) behind its rotation, part 4's pulse one frame behind, and the changes of events 201, 401, 403 and 501
  integrated from the capture's frame before the event (7..24 ms before the row). At another frame rate the
  original differs by that much.
- **3D engine (parts 7..10).** Faces are ordered by a depth buffer instead of the painter's sort into 4096 depth
  buckets (the same picture in every checked frame; the faces inside part 9's blocks, which only the sort can show,
  stay hidden). Perspective is exact per pixel (the original divides every 8 pixels), the bilinear weights, the shade
  and its exponential decode are continuous instead of 4-bit weights, 16.16 steps and a byte table: 0.7..2.2 levels
  on average on the dumped frames. Where a map is minified (part 8's fly-in, small cubes) it is filtered to its mean
  instead of aliasing into speckle. The maps' smooth style has round spots where the original's integer radius gives
  stepped rings (map C about 5 % darker on average). A triangle that crosses the edge of the window is shaded
  affinely on the screen as a whole; the original re-interpolates the shade where its clipper cuts it. The level-4
  geosphere with its integer vertices is kept (a finer one changes the shading of part 7's bumps).
- **Frame-rate effects of the 3D parts, kept**: part 9's turning axis switches at the capture's timer values (the
  frame before each event 902 frame, 0.07..2.6 t70 units before the row; at the rows the block would turn up to 5°
  differently), and part 7's motion stops where the capture's does (t70 9956.198, the frame before 28:00). Their
  tile colours are not truncated to bytes (half a step brighter on average).
- **The voxel grids' bugs, kept**: part 9's neighbour test reads x + 2 instead of x + 1; part 10's field array is
  laid out 6 × 6 × 6 while its loop runs to 10 in one axis, so the bottom of its column repeats the top shifted
  (24 % of the cubes), and its neighbour test reads past the array into part 10's time and part 2's camera time.
  They shape what the original shows.
- **Particle parts (1, 6, 3).** Their look depends on the frame rate (spawns, trails, part 3's magnify-and-darken),
  so they are stepped at the capture's frame times (`src/scenes/particle_frames.h`, made from `full.flips` by
  `tools/particle_frames.py`) and their events take effect on the capture's frame (up to one frame after the row).
  The particles, counts and random numbers are then the capture's: the original's 8-bit pipeline driven by this
  state gives all 3,174 captured frames pixel for pixel. Between those frames the dots move with the part's clock and
  the trails (part 1) and the cloud (part 3) blend into the next frame; part 6's blinking and part 1's flash change
  on their frame. After a jump the trails are rebuilt from the last 107 frames (part 3's cloud from 64), which can
  differ from playing through by one 8-bit level.
- **Dune's radial blur** (parts 1, 6) runs on the original's 320 × 200 grid, in float with its in-place order kept:
  every pixel reads the processed value of the footprint pixels the original's pass reaches before it and the input
  value of the others, iterated until it converges to the sequential pass (bit for bit in integer arithmetic,
  `tools/particlecheck.cpp`). In float it is 1.5 levels from the original's pass on average (at most 9 in a frame),
  where order-free versions are off by up to 40 at the strongest pulses; its streaks are added to full-resolution
  stamps. On a finer grid the recursion near the centre would no longer be the original's.
- **Stamps and buffers.** The stamps are smooth sprites at sub-pixel positions, clipped at the edges instead of
  dropped; the truncations of the feedback, the blur and part 3 become their mean losses; part 3's nearest-pixel
  magnification is filtered and its 4-tap blur a Gaussian, so its cloud is smoother than the original's.
